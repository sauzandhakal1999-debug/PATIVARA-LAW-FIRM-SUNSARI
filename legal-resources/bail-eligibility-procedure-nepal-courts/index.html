Bail Eligibility, Hearings, and Procedures (Thunseka) in Nepal Courts

📅 Published: 2025-02-25
🔄 Updated: 2025-10-01
⏱ 9 min read
🔍 Review: Senior Advocate Lead Counsel, Supreme Court Bar Association
Direct Legal Answer & Overview

Bail in Nepal is determined during the preliminary hearing (Thunseka Bahas / थुनछेक बहस) following the filing of the formal Charge-Sheet (Abhiyog-patra) by the Public Prosecutor. Under Sections 67, 68, and 69 of the Muluki Criminal Procedure Code 2074, the court decides whether to release the accused on a general court date (Sadharan Tarekh), release them on monetary bail (Dharauti) or bank guarantee (Jamanat), or remand them to judicial custody (Pukpak) awaiting trial.

Key Practical Takeaways

  • Bail hearings occur immediately after the formal Charge-Sheet is submitted in District Court.
  • Sections 67, 68, and 69 govern the statutory criteria for detention vs. bail.
  • Denial of bail or excessive bail amounts can be appealed to the High Court under Section 73.

The preliminary hearing—widely termed Thunseka Bahas (थुनछेक बहस) in Nepalese legal practice—is often the most crucial day of a criminal case. During this hearing, the court decides whether the accused will face trial as a free person on bail or remain confined in judicial custody (Pukpak) for months or years.

1. The Three Statutory Categories: Sections 67, 68 & 69

Under the Muluki Criminal Procedure Code 2074, judicial discretion regarding bail is structured into three clear statutory tiers:

  • Section 67 (Detention during Trial): For grave offenses punishable by imprisonment for three years or more, or life imprisonment (such as homicide, rape, large-scale narcotics, treason), the court generally remands the accused to judicial custody if credible prima facie evidence exists.
  • Section 68 (Release on Bail / Dharauti): For offenses where imprisonment is under three years, or where evidence does not firmly establish grave culpability, the court releases the accused upon depositing cash bail (Dharauti) or a bank guarantee.
  • Section 69 (Release on General Date / Sadharan Tarekh): For minor offenses punishable by imprisonment under six months or a fine, the accused is typically released without monetary bail on a simple promise to attend trial dates.

2. How the Bail Hearing (Thunseka) Unfolds

Once the Public Prosecutor files the Charge-Sheet (Abhiyog-patra), the judge reads the charges to the accused and records their statement in open court (Bayan). Following the statement, the Government Attorney argues for detention, and the defense advocate presents arguments demonstrating lack of flight risk, strong family ties, weak evidentiary links, and medical vulnerabilities.

3. Challenging Custody Orders: Section 73 High Court Review

If the District Court issues an order remanding the accused to judicial custody or demands an excessively burdensome bail amount, the accused is not without remedy. Under Section 73 of the Muluki Criminal Procedure Code 2074, the defense advocate can immediately file an interlocutory appeal before the High Court seeking to overturn the detention order and secure release on bail.

Frequently Asked Questions on this Topic

Can a foreign citizen get bail in a criminal case in Nepal?

Foreign citizens are eligible for bail under Nepalese law, but courts evaluate flight risk rigorously. Often, courts require substantial cash deposits, surrender of passports, and reliable Nepalese citizen guarantors (Jamanatdars).
