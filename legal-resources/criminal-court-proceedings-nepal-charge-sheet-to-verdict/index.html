Criminal Trial Procedures in Nepal: From Charge-Sheet (Abhiyog-patra) to Verdict

📅 Published: 2025-03-05
🔄 Updated: 2025-10-01
⏱ 9 min read
🔍 Review: Senior Advocate Lead Counsel, Supreme Court Bar Association
Direct Legal Answer & Overview

A criminal trial in Nepal proceeds through five structured stages under the Muluki Criminal Procedure Code 2074: 1) Filing of the Charge-Sheet (Abhiyog-patra) by the Government Attorney; 2) Recording of the accused statement (Bayan) and preliminary detention/bail hearing (Thunseka); 3) Presentation of prosecution evidence and defense witness examination (Praman Parikshan); 4) Final oral arguments (Bahas) on culpability; and 5) Judicial verdict (Faisala), followed by a separate statutory sentencing hearing within 30 days if convicted.

Key Practical Takeaways

  • Sentencing is now conducted as a separate post-verdict proceeding under modern Nepalese criminal procedure.
  • Cross-examination of prosecution police witnesses and forensic doctors is essential for testing credibility.
  • Acquittals or convictions can be appealed to the High Court within statutory appeal periods.

Following the enactment of the Muluki Criminal Procedure Code 2074 and the Sentencing Act 2074, criminal trials in Nepal adopted a modern two-tier structure: bifurcating the determination of guilt (verdict) from the determination of sentence (punishment).

1. The Charge-Sheet (Abhiyog-patra) & Court Registration

The criminal trial formally commences when the District Government Attorney Office (Sarkari Vakil Karyalaya) files the formal Charge-Sheet (Abhiyog-patra) in District Court. The charge-sheet sets out the alleged offense, specific statutory provisions under the Muluki Criminal Code 2074, names of accused persons, and a comprehensive list of evidence and witnesses.

2. Evidentiary Stage: Witness Examination and Cross-Examination (Jirah)

During the evidentiary phase, the prosecution produces its witnesses in open court: investigating police officers, eyewitnesses, seizure witnesses (Baramad Muchulka), and medical or forensic specialists. The defense advocate exercises the critical constitutional right of cross-examination (Jirah) to highlight inconsistencies, biases, procedural violations, or alternative explanations.

3. Bifurcated Sentencing under the Sentencing Act 2074

Under historic Nepalese law, judges announced guilt and sentence in a single stroke. Under the modern Sentencing Act 2074, if the judge finds the accused guilty, a separate sentencing hearing must be held within 30 days. During this second hearing, the court evaluates mitigating factors: first-time offender status, age, family dependency, remorse, and voluntary restitution, ensuring proportional sentencing.

  • ✔ Transcripts of all recorded witness statements
  • ✔ Medical, post-mortem, or forensic lab analysis reports

Frequently Asked Questions on this Topic

How long does a criminal trial take in Nepal District Courts?

While statutory guidelines target conclusion within six months to a year, complex multi-defendant criminal trials typically span between 8 and 18 months depending on witness attendance and forensic test turnaround.
