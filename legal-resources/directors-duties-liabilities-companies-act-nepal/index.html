Fiduciary Duties and Statutory Liabilities of Directors under Companies Act 2063

📅 Published: 2025-06-05
🔄 Updated: 2025-10-01
⏱ 9 min read
🔍 Review: Managing Associate Advocate, High Court Bar Association
Direct Legal Answer & Overview

Company directors in Nepal carry strict fiduciary duties under Chapter 6 of the Companies Act 2063 (कम्पनी ऐन, २०६३): 1) Duty of loyalty to act in the best interests of the company; 2) Duty of care, skill, and diligence; 3) Mandatory written disclosure of personal conflicts of interest (Section 92); 4) Prohibition against engaging in competing business (Section 101); and 5) Ensuring compliance with mandatory annual returns and tax filings. Breaching these duties triggers personal civil liability for company losses and potential disqualification from holding directorships.

Key Practical Takeaways

  • Directors owe fiduciary loyalty to the corporate entity, not to individual shareholders.
  • Conflicts of interest must be disclosed in writing at the earliest board meeting.
  • Personal liability attaches if directors act beyond corporate powers (Ultra Vires) or engage in fraudulent transactions.

Serving as a member of the Board of Directors of a company in Nepal is an honorable role—but one laden with serious statutory liability. Directors are not mere figureheads; under the Companies Act 2063, they stand in a strict fiduciary relationship as trustees of corporate assets and guardians of shareholder capital.

1. The Core Fiduciary Duties under Section 99

Under Section 99 of the Companies Act 2063, directors must perform their functions with reasonable care, skill, and diligence. They must act in good faith to promote the success of the enterprise for the benefit of its shareholders as a whole, while considering the interests of employees, creditors, and community welfare.

2. Mandatory Disclosure of Conflicts of Interest (Section 92)

If a director is directly or indirectly interested in any contract or commercial arrangement entered into by the company (e.g. leasing personal property to the company or purchasing goods from an entity owned by a spouse), the director must disclose the nature and extent of that interest in writing at the first board meeting. The interested director is statutorily barred from voting on the resolution.

3. When Can Directors be Held Personally Liable?

The corporate veil shields shareholders, but does not grant directors immunity from wrongdoing. Directors face personal financial liability and regulatory sanctions if they:

  • Authorize actions beyond the company statutory powers (Ultra Vires);
  • Distribute dividends out of capital when the company lacks net distributable profits;
  • Conceal corporate financial insolvency to defraud creditors; or
  • Fail to file mandatory annual returns, audited balance sheets, and tax remittances, triggering progressive statutory penalties under Section 160.

Frequently Asked Questions on this Topic

Can a director of a Private Limited Company operate a competing business in Nepal?

Under Section 101 of the Companies Act 2063, a director is prohibited from engaging in any business that competes with the company, or utilizing corporate property, information, or business opportunities for personal gain without the explicit approval of the general meeting.
