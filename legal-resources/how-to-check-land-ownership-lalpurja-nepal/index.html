How to Verify Land Ownership and Title Records (Lalpurja) in Nepal

📅 Published: 2025-04-15
🔄 Updated: 2025-10-01
⏱ 9 min read
🔍 Review: Senior Advocate Lead Counsel, Supreme Court Bar Association
Direct Legal Answer & Overview

To verify land ownership in Nepal: 1) Inspect the original Land Ownership Certificate (Lalpurja / लालपुर्जा) for official watermarks, district seal, and signatures; 2) Cross-verify the plot number (Kitta number), area, and owner name against the permanent Shrestha register at the Land Revenue Office (Malpot Karyalaya); 3) Inspect the cadastral Field Book and Trace Map at the Survey Office (Napi); 4) Conduct a formal Encumbrance/Freeze search (Rokka Talasi) to confirm absence of bank liens or court freezes; and 5) Confirm land is not classified as Guthi or government forest reserve.

Key Practical Takeaways

  • Never rely solely on a physical Lalpurja; official ledger records at Malpot hold ultimate legal authority.
  • Verify both Malpot (ownership ledger) and Napi (physical boundary map) records.
  • Confirm Rokka status to prevent purchasing bank-mortgaged or court-frozen land.

In Nepal, land is the ultimate store of generational wealth. However, real estate transactions are fraught with peril due to dual agency administration: the Survey Office (Napi Karyalaya) maps physical land boundaries, while the Land Revenue Office (Malpot Karyalaya) records ownership titles. Relying merely on a physical certificate without institutional verification is a recipe for fraud.

1. Step 1: Physical Examination of the Lalpurja

A genuine Land Ownership Registration Certificate (Lalpurja) features security watermarks, official government embossing seals, the signature of the Land Revenue Officer (Malpot Adhikrit), and explicit entries including: District, Municipality/Rural Municipality, Ward number, Sheet number, Plot number (Kitta Number), and exact area measured in Bigha-Katha-Dhur (in Terai) or Ropani-Aana-Paisa-Daam (in Hills/Kathmandu).

2. Step 2: The Critical Malpot Shrestha Cross-Check

A physical certificate can be forged, or superseded by subsequent transfers. Under the Land Revenue Act 2034, the permanent legal record is the Shrestha (श्रेस्ता) ledger maintained inside the Malpot vault. A formal legal search verifies:

  • Whether the person holding the Lalpurja is registered as the current lawful owner;
  • How the seller acquired the land (Sale deed, inheritance partition, or gift deed); and
  • Whether any unresolved heir disputes or mutation claims are noted in the margins.

3. Step 3: Checking for Rokka (Liens & Court Freezes)

A Rokka (रोक्का) is a statutory restriction that prevents transfer. Common reasons for Rokka include: commercial bank loan collateral, court injunctions during ongoing litigation, or CIAA asset freezes. A formal Rokka search must confirm the land is completely clear (Phukuwa).

4. Step 4: Survey Office (Napi) Cadastral Alignment

Finally, visit the Survey Office (Napi Karyalaya) with the plot number to obtain an official Blue Print and Trace Map. Verify that the plot on paper matches the actual physical boundaries on the ground, and confirm that access roads are recognized public rights-of-way rather than private encroachments.

Statutory Citations & Governing Nepal Legislation

Statute / Act Section / Rule Legal Significance
Land Revenue Act 2034 Section 4 & 5 (Shrestha Maintenance) Legal authority of Land Revenue Office registers over physical title certificates.
Survey and Measurement Act 2019 Section 6 (Cadastral Mapping) Survey boundary demarcation and trace map authenticity.

Documentary Checklist for this Procedure

  • ✔ Original Land Ownership Registration Certificate (Lalpurja)
  • ✔ Cadastral Blue Print / Trace Map issued by Survey Office
  • ✔ Latest land revenue tax payment receipts (Tiro Tireko Rasid)
  • ✔ Malpot Shrestha certified search copy

Frequently Asked Questions on this Topic

What should I do if the land area on the Lalpurja doesn't match the ground area in Nepal?

Discrepancies between title area and physical ground area require applying to the local Survey Office (Napi) for an official field re-measurement (Kshetraphal Sudhar). Never execute a purchase deed until area discrepancies are formally corrected in official records.
