Land Registration and Deed Transfer (Rajinama) in Nepal: Malpot Office Guide

📅 Published: 2025-04-25
🔄 Updated: 2025-10-01
⏱ 8 min read
🔍 Review: Senior Advocate Lead Counsel, Supreme Court Bar Association
Direct Legal Answer & Overview

Transferring real estate title in Nepal requires executing a formal Sale Deed (Rajinama Likhit / राजीनामा लिखत) at the competent Land Revenue Office (Malpot Karyalaya). The procedure involves: 1) Drafting the deed in statutory format; 2) Submitting buyer and seller KYC documents and photographs; 3) Paying statutory municipal registration fees and Capital Gains Tax (CGT); 4) Physical appearance before the Land Revenue Officer for biometric verification and statement recording (Sanaakhat); and 5) Mutation of records (Dakhil Kharij) and issuance of the fresh Lalpurja in the buyer name.

Key Practical Takeaways

  • Registration fee scales depend on municipal government valuation tariffs.
  • Sellers must pay Capital Gains Tax (CGT) based on ownership holding period (under vs. over 5 years).
  • Both buyer and seller must appear in person before the Malpot Officer unless holding an official court power of attorney.

Executing a real estate transfer in Nepal is a formal administrative process overseen by the Land Revenue Office (Malpot Karyalaya). Under Section 36 of the Land Revenue Act 2034, no land transfer has legal effect until formally registered in the government ledger (Shrestha).

1. Drafting the Official Sale Deed (Rajinama Likhit)

The deed must be drafted by a licensed deed writer (Lekhapadhak) or advocate in strict adherence to government templates. It specifies: full identities of buyer and seller, plot number, sheet number, municipal valuation, actual agreed consideration, boundaries, and clear covenants guaranteeing title freedom from prior encumbrances.

2. Government Registration Fees & Capital Gains Tax (CGT)

Before entering the Malpot verification hall, parties must pay statutory fees at the on-site bank counter:

  • Registration Tariff: Calculated as a percentage of the higher of the government minimum valuation or declared purchase price (commonly ranging between 2% and 5% depending on metropolitan, municipal, or rural location).
  • Capital Gains Tax (CGT): Paid by the seller if the property appreciated in value—typically 5% if held for more than 5 years, or 7.5% if held for less than 5 years.

3. In-Person Verification (Sanaakhat) & Title Mutation

Both buyer and seller appear before the Land Revenue Officer. The officer verifies identities, inspects original citizenship certificates, confirms that consideration was exchanged, captures biometric fingerprints and digital photographs, and records the seller statement (Sanaakhat). The registrar endorses the deed, enters the transfer into the digital Shrestha, and delivers the newly minted Lalpurja to the buyer.

Statutory Citations & Governing Nepal Legislation

Statute / Act Section / Rule Legal Significance
Land Revenue Act 2034 Section 36 & 37 (Deed Registration) Statutory mandate requiring registration of deeds for legal validity of real estate transfer.
Income Tax Act 2058 Section 95Ka (Capital Gains Tax) Withholding tax rates on sale of real estate.

Documentary Checklist for this Procedure

  • ✔ Original Lalpurja of the seller
  • ✔ Citizenship Certificates of buyer and seller
  • ✔ Official Rajinama Deed drafted in legal format
  • ✔ Bank vouchers for registration fee and Capital Gains Tax deposit

Frequently Asked Questions on this Topic

Can someone execute a land transfer if they are abroad via Power of Attorney?

Yes, but only through a formal Power of Attorney (Warisnama) certified and executed before the competent Nepalese Embassy abroad and formally endorsed by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA) and District Court in Nepal.
