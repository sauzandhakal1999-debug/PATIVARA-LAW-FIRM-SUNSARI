Matrimonial Property Division (Ansha) and Partition Rights on Divorce in Nepal

📅 Published: 2025-04-05
🔄 Updated: 2025-10-01
⏱ 9 min read
🔍 Review: Senior Advocate Lead Counsel, Supreme Court Bar Association
Direct Legal Answer & Overview

Under Section 99 of the Muluki Civil Code 2074, if a divorce occurs due to the fault of the husband, the wife holds a statutory right to claim her partition share (Ansha) from the husband property prior to the divorce decree being finalized. If the husband has not yet partitioned his ancestral estate, the court requires a deemed partition to calculate his share, from which the wife receives her equal portion. Alternatively, parties may mutually agree on a lump-sum financial settlement or periodic maintenance.

Key Practical Takeaways

  • Wife partition rights on divorce depend on statutory fault under Section 99.
  • Deemed ancestral partition (Mano Chhudaune) is calculated if husband holdings are unpartitioned.
  • Parties can negotiate lump-sum financial settlements in lieu of physical land partition.

Financial security post-divorce is one of the most critical aspects of matrimonial litigation in Nepal. Under Section 99 of the Muluki Civil Code 2074, the legal system protects the economic well-being of the wife by linking the granting of divorce to fair property partition (Ansha).

1. When Does the Right to Ansha Arise on Divorce?

Under Section 99(1), if a divorce is instituted because of the fault of the husband (e.g. abandonment, cruelty, adultery, or expelling the wife), the wife is entitled to claim her partition share before the divorce decree is granted. The court will not grant final divorce without first securing the wife rightful property share or recording an agreed financial settlement.

2. Deemed Ancestral Partition Mechanism

In many Nepalese households, ancestral property remains unpartitioned among parents and brothers. If the husband has not formally separated his share (Anshabanda), Section 99(2) mandates that the court must calculate the husband deemed share from the family estate, and from that calculated portion, allocate an equal share to the wife.

3. Alternative: Lump-Sum Financial Compensation or Maintenance

Dividing physical real estate often creates practical headaches. Under Section 99(5) and Section 100, spouses can agree to a lump-sum financial settlement (Ek-mushta Rakam) or monthly maintenance (Masik Kharacha) based on the husband financial income and standard of living, allowing both parties a clean economic break.

Frequently Asked Questions on this Topic

Does a wife lose her partition rights if divorce is due to her fault?

Under Section 99(3), if divorce occurs due to the fault of the wife (such as proven adultery or unprovoked abandonment), she is not entitled to claim partition from the husband property, though child support obligations remain unaffected.
