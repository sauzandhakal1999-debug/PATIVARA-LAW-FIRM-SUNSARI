Fundamental Rights of an Accused Person under the Constitution of Nepal

📅 Published: 2025-03-01
🔄 Updated: 2025-10-01
⏱ 8 min read
🔍 Review: Senior Advocate Lead Counsel, Supreme Court Bar Association
Direct Legal Answer & Overview

Article 20 of the Constitution of Nepal guarantees ten non-negotiable fundamental rights to every person accused of a crime: 1) Right to be informed of arrest grounds; 2) Right to legal counsel of choice; 3) Mandatory production before a judge within 24 hours; 4) Protection against ex-post facto penal laws; 5) Presumption of innocence; 6) Protection against double jeopardy; 7) Right against self-incrimination; 8) Right to a public trial; 9) Right to legal aid for indigent persons; and 10) Fair trial procedures.

Key Practical Takeaways

  • The presumption of innocence is the cornerstone of criminal jurisprudence in Nepal.
  • Evidence procured through custodial torture is inadmissible under statutory law.
  • Indigent persons have a constitutional right to state-funded legal aid representation.

A civilized criminal justice system is measured by how it treats individuals accused of crimes. Under Article 20 of the Constitution of Nepal, the legal system guarantees an explicit charter of procedural rights designed to ensure that no innocent person is wrongfully punished.

1. The Presumption of Innocence (निर्दोषिताको अनुमान)

Under Article 20(5), every person accused of an offense is presumed innocent until proven guilty by a competent judicial court. The burden of proving every element of the crime beyond reasonable doubt rests exclusively on the prosecuting state authority. An accused person is never required to prove their innocence.

2. Protection Against Custodial Coercion and Torture

Under Article 22 of the Constitution, no person in custody shall be subjected to physical or mental torture or cruel treatment. Furthermore, under Section 9 of the Evidence Act 2031, any confession or admission obtained through physical threat, inducement, or coercion is legally void and cannot be relied upon by the court to convict.

3. Double Jeopardy and Ex-Post Facto Laws

Under Article 20(4) and (6):

  • No Ex-Post Facto Law: No person can be penalized for an act that was not an offense under the law in force at the time the act was committed.
  • Double Jeopardy (दोहोरो खतरा विरुद्धको हक): No person shall be prosecuted and punished more than once for the exact same offense in a court of law.

Frequently Asked Questions on this Topic

Can a police officer question a suspect without their lawyer in Nepal?

While police can ask questions, Article 20(2) gives the accused the right to communicate with and have their advocate present during formal interrogations. If the accused requests their lawyer, questioning should pause until counsel is contacted.
