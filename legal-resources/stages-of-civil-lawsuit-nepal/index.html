Stages of a Civil Lawsuit in Nepal: From Plaint Filing to Decree Execution

📅 Published: 2025-01-20
🔄 Updated: 2025-10-01
⏱ 9 min read
🔍 Review: Senior Advocate Lead Counsel, Supreme Court Bar Association
Direct Legal Answer & Overview

A civil lawsuit in Nepal under the Muluki Civil Procedure Code 2074 proceeds through six sequential stages: 1) Institution and Registration of the Plaint (Firdi-patra Darta); 2) Issuance and Service of Summons (Myad Tameli) on defendants; 3) Filing of the Written Defence (Pratiuttar-patra); 4) Evidence Submission and Witness Examination (Praman Parikshan); 5) Final Legal Hearing and Judgment (Bahas ra Faisala); and 6) Execution of Court Decree (Faisala Karyanvayan).

Key Practical Takeaways

  • Service of judicial summons (Myad Tameli) is strictly regulated; defendants have 21 days (extendable by 15 days) to respond.
  • Written statements of defence must dispute specific plaint allegations or face deemed admission.
  • Witnesses must appear in court to record oral statements and submit to cross-examination (Jirah).
  • Execution of decrees (Faisala Karyanvayan) is a separate procedural process initiated after judgment.

Navigating a civil lawsuit in Nepal requires a comprehensive understanding of statutory court procedures. Under the Muluki Civil Procedure Code 2074, civil trials follow a strict procedural sequence designed to ensure natural justice, equal opportunity to be heard, and procedural finality.

Stage 1: Drafting, Verification & Plaint Registration (Firdi Darta)

The litigation journey begins when the aggrieved party (Plaintiff / Firdi), assisted by a licensed advocate, prepares the formal Plaint (Firdi-patra). The plaint must specify:

  • The factual cause of action and date when the right was violated;
  • Calculations demonstrating that the case falls within statutory limitation (Hadmyad);
  • The territorial and subject-matter jurisdiction of the court;
  • The specific legal claim and valuation of the dispute; and
  • Mandatory court fees paid pursuant to the Court Fee Act 2017.

Once verified by the court registrar (Sarishtedar), the case is assigned a formal registration number (Darta Number).

Stage 2: Issuance & Service of Court Summons (Myad Tameli)

Following registration, the court issues a judicial summons (Myad) requiring the defendant to submit their defence. The court process server (Tameldar) delivers the notice to the defendant residence. If the defendant cannot be found or evades service, the notice is formally posted at the local municipal ward office in the presence of ward representatives (Riti Purvak Myad Tameli).

Stage 3: Written Statement of Defence (Pratiuttar-patra)

Upon receiving summons, the defendant has a statutory period of 21 days (which may be extended once by up to 15 days upon formal application under Section 106) to file their Written Statement of Defence (Pratiuttar-patra). Any allegation in the plaint not specifically denied in the defence is legally deemed admitted by the court.

Stage 4: Evidence Submission & Witness Examination (Praman Parikshan)

Once pleadings close, the judge identifies the legal issues in dispute. Both sides submit certified documentary exhibits (such as contracts, Lalpurja, bank statements, or official survey traces). Witnesses listed in the pleadings appear in person before the court to record their testimony under oath, followed by rigorous cross-examination (Jirah) by opposing counsel.

Stage 5: Final Hearing & Judgment (Bahas ra Faisala)

Both advocates deliver comprehensive oral arguments (Bahas), analyzing statutory sections, case precedents of the Supreme Court of Nepal (NPR / Nepal Kanoon Patrika), and documentary evidence. The judge renders the final judgment (Faisala), detailing the reasoning and decrees awarded.

Stage 6: Execution of Decree (Faisala Karyanvayan)

A winning judgment does not automatically transfer money or land. The victorious party must file a petition for Decree Execution (Faisala Karyanvayan) within the limitation period. The court execution wing executes the judgment through bank account freezing, property auction, or police-assisted eviction.

Frequently Asked Questions on this Topic

How long does a civil suit take from filing to judgment in District Courts?

While procedural codes prescribe targeted timelines within one year, contested civil cases in Kathmandu or Sunsari courts typically take between 12 and 24 months depending on appeals, summons service delays, and court caseload.

Can a defendant file an appeal against an adverse District Court civil judgment?

Yes. Under Section 216 of the Muluki Civil Procedure Code 2074, an aggrieved party can file an appeal (Punaravedan) before the competent High Court within 30 days of the judgment decree, extendable by 15 days for reasonable cause.
