Step-by-Step Guide to Registering a Company at the OCR in Nepal

📅 Published: 2025-05-15
🔄 Updated: 2025-10-01
⏱ 9 min read
🔍 Review: Managing Associate Advocate, High Court Bar Association
Direct Legal Answer & Overview

Incorporating a Private Limited Company in Nepal under the Companies Act 2063 involves five sequential steps: 1) Online name reservation on the Office of Company Registrar (OCR) portal; 2) Drafting customized Memorandum of Association (Prabandhapatra) and Articles of Association (Niyamawali); 3) Digital submission of founder KYC and charter documents; 4) Payment of progressive government registration stamp fees; and 5) Issuance of the Certificate of Incorporation, followed by Inland Revenue PAN/VAT and municipal ward registration within 30 days.

Key Practical Takeaways

  • Registration is fully digitalized via the OCR web portal (www.ocr.gov.np).
  • Statutory registration fees scale progressively based on authorized capital.
  • Post-incorporation tax (PAN/VAT) and municipal ward registration are legally mandatory before operations begin.

Establishing a corporate entity in Nepal provides founders with limited liability, perpetual succession, and an institutional platform to raise capital. Governed by the Companies Act 2063, the Office of Company Registrar (OCR / कम्पनी रजिस्ट्रारको कार्यालय) oversees all company incorporations through its online portal.

Step 1: Digital Name Reservation

The incorporation process begins by creating an account on the OCR digital portal (www.ocr.gov.np) and submitting your proposed company name. The name must be distinctive, accurately reflect the business objective, and must not conflict with existing trademarks or registered company names. OCR approval typically takes 2 to 4 working days.

Step 2: Drafting Charter Documents (MOA & AOA)

The constitutional documents of your company must be drafted in Nepali adhering to statutory formats:

  • Memorandum of Association (प्रबन्धपत्र - Prabandhapatra): Defines the fundamental objectives of the company, authorized and paid-up capital, liability limits, and the initial share commitments of promoters.
  • Articles of Association (नियमावली - Niyamawali): Governs internal management, board meeting quorums, directorship qualifications, share transfer rules, and dividend protocols.

Step 3: Document Submission & Statutory Fee Payment

Upload the verified MOA, AOA, and promoter identification documents to the OCR portal. Government registration fees scale progressively based on authorized capital (starting from NPR 1,000 for small capitalizations up to tiered brackets for larger enterprises).

Step 4: Certificate of Incorporation Issuance

Upon reviewing the submission, the OCR issues the digital Certificate of Incorporation (दर्ताको प्रमाणपत्र). The company now legally exists as an independent artificial person capable of owning assets, entering into contracts, and suing or being sued in its own name.

Step 5: Post-Registration Compliance (PAN/VAT & Ward)

Incorporation is not the final step. Within 30 days, the company must: 1) Open a corporate bank account; 2) Obtain a Permanent Account Number (PAN/VAT) from the competent Inland Revenue Office; 3) Register with the local Municipal Ward Office; and 4) File initial director and shareholder notification forms (Form 9 & 10) with the OCR.

Statutory Citations & Governing Nepal Legislation

Statute / Act Section / Rule Legal Significance
Companies Act 2063 Section 4 & 5 (Incorporation of Company) Legal framework for application, charter documents, and registration requirements.
Companies Act 2063 Section 17 (Certificate of Incorporation) Conclusive evidence of company legal personality and incorporation.

Documentary Checklist for this Procedure

  • ✔ Citizenship Certificate or Passport copies of all promoters
  • ✔ Passport-sized photographs of all promoters and directors
  • ✔ Draft Memorandum and Articles of Association (MOA & AOA)
  • ✔ Rent agreement and municipal property proof for registered office

Frequently Asked Questions on this Topic

What is the minimum capital required to register a Private Limited Company in Nepal?

Under the Companies Act 2063, there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement for a standard domestic Private Limited Company (except for specialized sectors like banking, insurance, or tourism). However, for Foreign Direct Investment (FDI), statutory minimum thresholds established under FITTA 2075 apply.
