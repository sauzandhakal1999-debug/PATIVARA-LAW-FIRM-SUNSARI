What to Know When Contacted by Nepal Police: Your Constitutional Rights Explained

📅 Published: 2025-02-15
🔄 Updated: 2025-10-01
⏱ 8 min read
🔍 Review: Senior Advocate Lead Counsel, Supreme Court Bar Association
Direct Legal Answer & Overview

If contacted, questioned, or summoned by the Nepal Police, remember four crucial constitutional rights under Article 20 of the Constitution of Nepal: 1) The right to be informed immediately of the reason and grounds for questioning or arrest; 2) The absolute right to consult and be defended by a licensed advocate of your choice; 3) The right to remain silent and not be compelled to give self-incriminating statements; and 4) The mandatory requirement that any arrested person must be produced before a judicial authority within 24 hours.

Key Practical Takeaways

  • Never sign blank papers or unread statement transcripts (Kagazat).
  • You have the right to have your licensed advocate present during formal interrogation.
  • Police custody without producing the person before a judge within 24 hours violates the Constitution of Nepal.

Being contacted, summoned to a police post (Prahari Chauki), or visited by investigating officers of the Nepal Police is an intimidating experience. However, knowing your statutory rights under the Constitution of Nepal and the Muluki Criminal Procedure Code 2074 ensures that you do not compromise your legal defense.

1. Understand the Difference: Informal Inquiry vs. Formal Summon vs. Arrest

Police contact generally falls into three legal tiers:

  • Informal Inquiry: Police seeking preliminary factual information regarding an incident. You are entitled to ask whether you are considered a witness or a suspect.
  • Formal Written Summons (हाजिर हुन आउने पत्र): Police issuing an official written order requesting your presence for investigation. You should immediately retain an advocate before attending.
  • Formal Arrest (पक्राउ): Deprivation of personal liberty. Police must formally deliver a written arrest notice (Pakrau Purji) stating the specific alleged offense.

2. Your Core Constitutional Protections under Article 20

The Constitution of Nepal enshrines vital procedural safeguards that no police official can lawfully override:

  1. Right to Know Arrest Grounds (Art. 20(1)): No person can be detained without being informed of the grounds of arrest.
  2. Right to Legal Counsel (Art. 20(2)): You have the fundamental right to consult with an advocate of your choice from the moment of arrest, and communications with your lawyer remain privileged and confidential.
  3. 24-Hour Production Mandate (Art. 20(3)): Every arrested person must be physically produced before the nearest judicial bench within 24 hours (excluding travel time).
  4. Right Against Self-Incrimination (Art. 20(7)): No person accused of an offense can be compelled to be a witness against themselves.

3. Golden Rules During Police Interrogation

Remain polite and composed. State your identity clearly. Never sign blank papers or statement drafts without reading every sentence carefully. If statements are recorded in Nepali that you cannot read or understand, demand a certified translation or refuse to sign until your advocate is present.

Frequently Asked Questions on this Topic

Can Nepal Police search my house without a search warrant?

Under Section 21 of the Muluki Criminal Procedure Code 2074, police generally require a formal Search Warrant (Talasi Purji). However, in urgent situations where evidence is at imminent risk of destruction, officers of specific rank may search with an emergency notice, but must prepare a formal seizure memo (Baramadka Muchulka) signed by local ward witnesses.
