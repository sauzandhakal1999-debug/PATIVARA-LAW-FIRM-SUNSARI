Understanding the Legal Problem & Client Need

Informal agreements, internet templates, or poorly drafted contracts frequently fail when put to the test in Nepalese courts. Under Part 5, Chapter 1 of the Muluki Civil Code 2074, agreements missing essential elements (such as lawful consideration, mutual consent, and legal capacity) are void ab initio. Ambiguous dispute resolution clauses, unaddressed force majeure events, or missing indemnity covenants can leave businesses exposed to severe financial loss and endless litigation.

Important Legal Rights, Duties & Procedural Limitations

  • • Void Agreements: Contracts contrary to public policy, restraining trade unlawfully, or lacking lawful consideration are void under Section 517 of the Civil Code.
  • • Limitation on Breach Suits: Suits claiming damages for breach of contract must be instituted within strict statutory limitation periods (generally 2 years from breach date).
  • • Specific Performance: Nepalese courts may decree specific performance only in cases where monetary compensation does not afford adequate relief, subject to statutory conditions.

Common Questions Regarding Contract Drafting & Agreement Review

Are electronic contracts and digital signatures legally enforceable in Nepal?

Yes. Under the Electronic Transactions Act 2063 (ETA 2063) and provisions of the Muluki Civil Code 2074, contracts formed through electronic communications and executed via verified digital or electronic signatures are legally valid and admissible as evidence in Nepalese courts.

What remedies are available if the other party breaches a commercial contract in Nepal?

The non-breaching party can pursue: 1) A suit for monetary compensation/damages (Kshatipurti) under Section 537 of the Civil Code; 2) Specific performance of the contract (where applicable); 3) Immediate contract termination and restitution; or 4) Interim court injunctions preventing further breach.
