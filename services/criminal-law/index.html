Criminal Law & Defence Representation Legal Services in Nepal

We offer vigilant, ethical criminal defence representation for individuals, corporate officers, and accused persons facing investigation or formal prosecution by the Nepal Police and Office of the Attorney General. From police questioning and judicial remand (Thap Myad) to bail hearings (Dharauti/Jamanat) and District Court trials, we safeguard your constitutional rights under the National Criminal Procedure Code 2074.

Understanding the Legal Problem & Client Need

Facing a criminal investigation or arrest in Nepal is an urgent, high-stakes crisis. The first 24 hours of detention are critical for asserting constitutional rights under Article 20 of the Constitution of Nepal, including the right to remain silent and the right to consult legal counsel without delay. Statements coerced without legal representation can jeopardize freedom. Navigating police custody, judicial remand extensions, bail hearings, and charge-sheet scrutiny requires deep technical knowledge of the Muluki Criminal Code and Criminal Procedure Code 2074.

  • ⚖Judicial remand hearings (Thap Myad) before the District Court within the statutory 24-hour window

Typical Legal Process & Procedural Stages in Nepal

Our advocates guide clients step-by-step through each statutory stage mandated by Nepalese court rules:

1

Immediate Counsel & Police Custody Representation

Visiting the detainee at the police station (Prahari Karyalaya), reviewing arrest grounds, and ensuring statements are given voluntarily and truthfully without coercion.
Estimated Timeline: Within 24 Hours of Arrest

2

Judicial Remand Hearings (Thap Myad)

Appearing before the District Court when police request custody extensions, opposing arbitrary detention, and requesting medical examinations.
Estimated Timeline: Investigation Period (Up to 25 to 60 days depending on offense)

3

Charge-Sheet Scrutiny & Preliminary Hearing (Thunseka)

Once the Government Attorney files the formal Charge-Sheet (Abhiyog-patra), presenting arguments on whether the accused should be released on bail, general date (Tarekh), or remanded to judicial custody (Pukpak).
Estimated Timeline: 1 – 3 Days upon Charge-Sheet Filing

4

Trial, Evidence & Cross-Examination

Submitting defense evidence, examining prosecution witnesses, contesting forensic reports, and proving absence of criminal intent (Mens Rea) or reasonable doubt.
Estimated Timeline: 4 – 12 Months

5

Final Arguments, Verdict & Appellate Review

Delivering final legal arguments before the bench, reviewing judgment rationale, and filing timely appeals before the High Court or Supreme Court.
Estimated Timeline: 1 – 3 Months post-verdict

Important Legal Rights, Duties & Procedural Limitations

  • • Strict Ethical Notice: In compliance with the Nepal Bar Council Code of Conduct, we strictly do not promise or guarantee bail grants, acquittals, or case dismissal. Decisions rest entirely with the judicial bench.
  • • 24-Hour Production Requirement: Under Article 20(3) of the Constitution of Nepal, every arrested person must be produced before the nearest judicial authority within 24 hours (excluding transit time).
  • • Right to Legal Consultation: The accused possesses an absolute constitutional right to consult and be defended by a licensed advocate of their choice from the moment of arrest.
  • • Presumption of Innocence: Every accused person is presumed innocent until proven guilty beyond reasonable doubt by a competent court of law.

Common Questions Regarding Criminal Law & Defence Representation

What should someone do immediately if arrested by Nepal Police?

The arrested person should politely state their name and identification, ask for the legal grounds of arrest, request immediate notification of their family or lawyer, and exercise their constitutional right under Article 20 not to be coerced into giving a self-incriminating statement until their advocate is present.

What factors do judges in Nepal consider when granting bail (Dharauti)?

Under Sections 67, 68, and 69 of the Muluki Criminal Procedure Code 2074, judges evaluate: the gravity of the alleged offense; maximum imprisonment prescribed; previous criminal record; risk of flight; risk of witness tampering or destruction of evidence; and the health, age, or physical condition of the accused.

Can an accused appeal if the District Court refuses bail and sends them to judicial custody?

Yes. Under Section 73 of the Muluki Criminal Procedure Code 2074, an order remanding the accused to judicial custody (Pukpak) or demanding an excessive bail amount can be challenged immediately before the competent High Court through an interlocutory appeal.

Can a criminal complaint in Nepal be withdrawn once filed?

For State-prosecuted crimes (Schedule 1 offenses like forgery, murder, or banking fraud), the individual complainant cannot simply withdraw the complaint. The Government of Nepal, represented by the Public Prosecutor, controls prosecution. In minor compoundable individual offenses, statutory reconciliation (Milapatra) may be accepted by the court.
