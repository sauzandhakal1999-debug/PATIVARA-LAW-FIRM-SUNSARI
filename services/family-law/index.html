Family Law, Divorce & Matrimonial Dispute Representation in Nepal

Family disputes require high legal precision handled with privacy and compassion. Our family law practice assists spouses and families navigating divorce (Sambandh Bichhed), child custody and guardianship, spousal maintenance (Kharacha), and matrimonial property partition (Ansha) under the Muluki Civil Code 2074. We represent clients in District Courts as well as confidential structured mediation.

Understanding the Legal Problem & Client Need

The dissolution of a marriage in Nepal touches deeply sensitive personal and economic realities. Under Part 3, Chapter 3 of the Muluki Civil Code 2074, both men and women possess explicit statutory grounds to initiate divorce before the District Court. Crucial legal issues frequently accompany divorce: determining whether a 1-year reconciliation period is mandatory, partition of matrimonial property (Anshabanda), child custody arrangements, and ongoing child maintenance. Without informed legal counsel, spouses risk forfeiting vital economic protections or experiencing protracted adversarial litigation.

Typical Legal Process & Procedural Stages in Nepal

Our advocates guide clients step-by-step through each statutory stage mandated by Nepalese court rules:

1

Confidential Consultation & Fact Assessment

Evaluating the marriage background, grounds for dissolution, presence of minor children, and identifying whether mutual consent or a contested petition is legally appropriate.
Estimated Timeline: 1 – 3 Days

2

Settlement Negotiation / Pleadings Formulation

Drafting the mutual agreement deed (Milapatra) or preparing the formal Plaint (Firdi-patra) with statutory grounds and property claims.
Estimated Timeline: 1 – 2 Weeks

3

District Court Filing & Formal Registration

Registering the petition at the competent District Court (where marriage took place, where wife resides, or where husband resides).
Estimated Timeline: 1 – 3 Working Days

4

Court Conciliation / Hearing Proceedings

For mutual consent: joint court appearance, recording of statements, and immediate decree. For contested suits: court-mandated 1-year mediation cooling period.
Estimated Timeline: 2 – 5 Days (Mutual) / 1 Year (Contested Mediation)

5

Final Decree, Property Partition & Custody Order

Issuance of the final divorce decree (Sambandh Bichhed Faisala), execution of property division, and issuance of formal certificate.
Estimated Timeline: 1 – 3 Weeks post-settlement

Important Legal Rights, Duties & Procedural Limitations

  • • 1-Year Conciliation Mandate: Under Section 97 of the Muluki Civil Code 2074, if a contested divorce petition is filed, the court must mandate a conciliation period of up to one year to attempt reconciliation before proceeding to final trial, unless statutory exceptions apply.
  • • Equal Right to Seek Divorce: Both husband and wife hold statutory rights to petition for divorce on specific enumerated grounds under the Civil Code.
  • • Best Interest of the Child: Under Nepalese law, child custody is not an absolute proprietary right of either parent; it is determined by the child age (under 5 typically resides with mother) and supreme welfare.
  • • Property Division Timing: If the wife is entitled to partition (Ansha) upon divorce, the property share must be calculated or lump-sum maintenance agreed before final dissolution.

Common Questions Regarding Family Law & Divorce Representation

How fast can a mutual consent divorce be completed in Nepal?

When both husband and wife mutually agree to divorce and execute a comprehensive settlement deed, the District Court can finalize the divorce decree within 2 to 5 working days from registration.

Can a husband file for divorce directly in Nepal under current law?

Yes. Under Section 94 of the Muluki Civil Code 2074, a husband can directly file for divorce in the District Court on grounds including: living separately for three or more consecutive years without consent, depriving of maintenance, committing physical or mental cruelty, or adultery.

How is child custody determined under Nepalese family law?

Under Section 115 of the Muluki Civil Code 2074: a child under five years of age typically remains under the custody of the mother unless otherwise agreed; a child above five years may remain with either parent based on mutual agreement or court assessment of the best welfare of the child. The non-custodial parent retains visitation rights and maintenance duties.

Is a wife entitled to property share (Ansha) upon divorce in Nepal?

Yes. Under Section 99 of the Muluki Civil Code 2074, if divorce occurs due to the fault of the husband, the wife is entitled to claim her partition share from the husband property before divorce is granted, or receive a lump-sum amount or periodic maintenance based on the husband income.
