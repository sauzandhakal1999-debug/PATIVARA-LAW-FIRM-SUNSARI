Ancestral Estate Rights & Succession Advisory

Inheritance, Succession & Ancestral Estate Legal Services in Nepal

Succession and ancestral property partition represent the most frequent civil disputes in Nepalese society. We assist heirs, surviving spouses, daughters, and families in determining lawful inheritance rights (Apunstrali / Hakwala), formalizing ancestral partition (Anshabanda), drafting wills (Ichhapatra), and executing property mutations under the Muluki Civil Code 2074.

Understanding the Legal Problem & Client Need

The enactment of the Muluki Civil Code 2074 fundamentally modernized inheritance law in Nepal, establishing equal inheritance rights for daughters and sons regardless of marital status. However, ancestral property (Paitrik Sampatti) disputes remain fraught with tension. Issues regularly arise over unpartitioned properties, concealed assets, fraudulent gift deeds (Bakaspatra), contested wills, or the rights of elderly parents to maintenance. Resolving inheritance matters requires reconciling complex genealogical lineages with strict statutory succession hierarchies.

Situations Where this Service is Relevant

  • ⚖Partition of ancestral estate (Anshabanda) among lawful co-heirs (Anshiyars)
  • ⚖Asserting equal inheritance rights of married or unmarried daughters under current law
  • ⚖Challenging fraudulent transfer or alienation of unpartitioned family property
  • ⚖Settling succession rights following the death of a property owner without a will (Apunstrali)
  • ⚖Drafting and registering legally binding Wills (Ichhapatra) or Family Settlement Deeds
  • ⚖Representation in property mutation and transfer of title at the Land Revenue Office (Malpot)

Scope of Legal Assistance Offered by our Chambers

  • ✔Negotiating and drafting formal Partition Deeds (Anshabanda Likhit) for registration at the Malpot Office
  • ✔Representing heirs in contested Partition Suits (Anshabanda Mudda) before District Courts

  • ✔Assisting non-resident Nepalese (NRNs) in claiming, protecting, or transferring ancestral property in Nepal

Typical Legal Process & Procedural Stages in Nepal

Our advocates guide clients step-by-step through each statutory stage mandated by Nepalese court rules:

1

Genealogical & Estate Assessment

Examining family relation certificates (Nata Pramanit), family tree (Bansawali), and listing all real estate and financial assets.
Estimated Timeline: 2 – 4 Days

2

Asset Search & Valuation

Verifying land title records (Lalpurja) at competent Malpot offices and identifying any concealed or encumbered properties.
Estimated Timeline: 1 – 2 Weeks

3

Amicable Partition Negotiation / Deed Drafting

Drafting fair partition shares complying with statutory equal-share mandates and presenting deed for co-heir approval.
Estimated Timeline: 1 – 3 Weeks

4

Malpot Registration / Court Litigation

Registering the agreed deed at the Land Revenue Office or, if contested, initiating a formal Anshabanda lawsuit in District Court.
Estimated Timeline: 1 Day (Deed) / 10 – 18 Months (Litigation)

5

Mutation & Individual Title Issuance

Securing separate Lalpurjas in each heir individual name following court decree or registered deed execution.
Estimated Timeline: 2 – 4 Weeks

Important Legal Rights, Duties & Procedural Limitations

  • • Equal Partition Rights: Under Section 205 of the Muluki Civil Code 2074, father, mother, husband, wife, son, and daughter are deemed equal co-heirs (Anshiyar) with equal shares in ancestral property.
  • • Care of Elderly Parents: Under statutory provisions, co-heirs taking their partition share are legally obligated to provide maintenance and care for elderly parents.
  • • Right of Self-Acquired Property: An individual possesses absolute liberty to bequeath or dispose of self-acquired property without partition constraints.

Common Questions Regarding Inheritance & Succession Law

Do married daughters have an equal right to ancestral property in Nepal?

Yes. Under the Muluki Civil Code 2074, both married and unmarried daughters hold equal coparcenary rights to ancestral property alongside sons, father, and mother. The previous restriction depriving married daughters of partition rights has been abolished.

What happens if a co-heir sells family land without the knowledge of other heirs?

If a co-heir alienates unpartitioned ancestral property without obtaining the written consent of all co-heirs, the aggrieved heirs can file a lawsuit for cancellation of deed (Likhit Badr Mudda) within statutory limitation periods to nullify the sale.
