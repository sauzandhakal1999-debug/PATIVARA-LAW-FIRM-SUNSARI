Labour & Employment Law Legal Advisory & Representation in Nepal

Understanding the Legal Problem & Client Need

Nepal labour jurisprudence enforces strict protections for employees and workers. Under the Labour Act 2074, employment cannot be terminated at will without strictly adhering to statutory disciplinary grounds, prior written warnings, and procedural due process. Failing to register with the Social Security Fund (SSF), misclassifying permanent workers as outsourced staff, or executing unlawful retrenchments exposes employers to substantial back-pay awards, reinstatement decrees, and statutory fines.

Important Legal Rights, Duties & Procedural Limitations

  • • Procedural Due Process Mandatory: Under Section 131 of the Labour Act 2074, an employer cannot terminate an employee for misconduct without issuing a formal show-cause notice providing at least seven days for written explanation.
  • • Mandatory SSF Registration: Employers are legally mandated to register all employees with the Social Security Fund from day one of employment.
  • • Probationary Period Limit: Statutory probation is strictly limited to six months under the Labour Act 2074; exceeding this timeline automatically confirms employment.

Common Questions Regarding Labour & Employment Law Representation

Can an employer terminate an employee without notice in Nepal?

No. Under the Labour Act 2074, termination without cause ('at-will') is prohibited. Termination can occur only on specific statutory grounds (such as grave misconduct, continued poor performance following notice, or lawful retrenchment approved by the Labour Office) with mandatory notice periods or pay in lieu.

Is enrollment in the Social Security Fund (SSF) compulsory for private companies?

Yes. Under the Social Security Act 2074 and government notifications, all registered commercial entities in Nepal are legally mandated to enroll their employees and remit monthly statutory contributions (20% employer and 11% employee).
