Property, Real Estate & Land Law Legal Services in Nepal

Land and real estate represent the most valuable asset class in Nepal, yet they are fraught with administrative complexity and inheritance disputes. We provide rigorous legal due diligence for property purchases, title deed verification (Lalpurja examination), representation at the Land Revenue Office (Malpot Karyalaya), ancestral partition litigation (Anshabanda Mudda), and boundary dispute resolution under the Land Act 2021 and Muluki Civil Code 2074.

Understanding the Legal Problem & Client Need

Purchasing or holding real estate in Nepal without comprehensive legal verification carries severe risks. Land records are maintained across dual governmental agencies: the Survey Office (Napi Karyalaya) for cadastral maps and the Land Revenue Office (Malpot Karyalaya) for ownership ledgers (Shrestha). Common pitfalls include purchasing land subject to an unregistered court freeze (Rokka), unpartitioned ancestral rights (Anshiyar share claims), Guthi trust restrictions, tenant tenancy claims (Mohi rights), or inaccurate boundary mappings. Once a fraudulent transfer occurs, rectifying title involves years of court litigation.

Typical Legal Process & Procedural Stages in Nepal

Our advocates guide clients step-by-step through each statutory stage mandated by Nepalese court rules:

1

Document Collection & Initial Review

Reviewing the client copy of the Land Ownership Registration Certificate (Lalpurja), citizenship, and property tax payment slips.
Estimated Timeline: 1 – 2 Days

2

Malpot & Survey Office On-Site Verification

Visiting the competent Land Revenue Office (Malpot) and Survey Office (Napi) to inspect the official register (Shrestha), verify Rokka status, and review the cadastral map.
Estimated Timeline: 3 – 7 Working Days

3

Due Diligence Report / Agreement Drafting

Delivering a formal Title Due Diligence Opinion and preparing the formal deed (Rajinama, Anshabanda, or Byna-patra) compliant with statutory formatting rules.
Estimated Timeline: 2 – 4 Days

4

Malpot Registration / Court Plaint Filing

For transactions: accompanying parties to the Malpot Office for biometric verification and registration. For disputes: filing the plaint in District Court.
Estimated Timeline: 1 Day (Transfer) / 2 – 4 Weeks (Litigation filing)

5

Title Mutation & Final Lalpurja Issuance

Obtaining the freshly mutated Lalpurja in the buyer or rightful heir name and updating municipal property tax records (Dakhil Kharij).
Estimated Timeline: 1 – 2 Weeks post-execution

Important Legal Rights, Duties & Procedural Limitations

  • • Mandatory Deed Registration: Under Section 36 of the Land Revenue Act 2034, any deed transferring title, gift, or partition of land must be registered at the Malpot Office to hold legal validity against third parties.
  • • Co-heir Partition Consent: Disposing of ancestral property without obtaining written consent or executing formal partition (Anshabanda) with all lawful heirs can result in the sale being legally voided in court.
  • • Ceiling Restrictions (Hadbandi): Individual and family landholdings in Nepal are subject to statutory acreage ceilings depending on geographic zones (Terai, Hills, Kathmandu Valley).
  • • Guthi Land Classifications: Rajguthi and private Guthi lands carry distinct statutory limitations and cannot be freely transferred without Guthi Sansthan clearance.

Common Questions Regarding Property & Land Law Representation

What is a 'Rokka' on land, and how can a buyer check for it in Nepal?

A 'Rokka' is a formal legal freeze placed on a land parcel in the Malpot Office records, typically by a commercial bank (as loan collateral), a judicial court (due to pending litigation), or the Commission for the Investigation of Abuse of Authority (CIAA). A buyer can verify Rokka status by conducting a formal search of the Shrestha ledger at the competent Land Revenue Office.

Can a father or mother sell ancestral property in Nepal without the consent of children?

Under Chapter 10 of the Muluki Civil Code 2074, parents cannot alienate ancestral property (Paitrik Sampatti) without the formal written consent of all adult coparceners (Anshiyars), except for necessary family survival or medical emergencies as strictly defined by statute. Self-acquired property (Swaparjita Sampatti), however, may be disposed of freely.

What is the legal difference between Bakaspatra (Gift Deed) and Rajinama (Sale Deed)?

A Rajinama is a registered commercial deed where ownership transfers for financial consideration (purchase price). A Bakaspatra is a registered voluntary gift deed transferring property without monetary exchange, commonly executed between family relations. Conditional gift deeds (Haldekhibako Bakaspatra) are irrevocable once executed in accordance with statutory conditions.

What should an owner do if a neighbor encroaches on their land boundary?

The owner should first apply to the local Survey Office (Napi Karyalaya) for official re-measurement and demarcation based on the permanent cadastral field book. If the encroachment persists, the owner can lodge an application with the local Municipal Ward Judicial Committee (Nyarik Samiti) or file an unlawful possession suit in District Court.
